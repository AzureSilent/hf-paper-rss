cmHC: Balancing Stream Mixing in Hyper-Connections via Geometric Contraction

Institution: XingChen-AGI
Authors: Haowei He, Ziwen Zhao, Chenshuo Pan, Gang Lin, Zhihao Yang
HF Page arXiv PDF
AI Summary
Abstract Constraining residual stream mixing within the Birkhoff polytope via geometric contraction improves stability and scalability of hyper-connections in large-scale dense and mixture-of-experts model pretraining. Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash
▼ Abstract (click to expand)

Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash Manifold-constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC) extends residual connections to multiple streams and stabilizes residual propagation by constraining stream-mixing residual matrices to the Birkhoff polytope, the set of doubly stochastic matrices. In this work, we investigate how to effectively mix residual streams within the Birkhoff polytope. We reveal a mixing trade-off: when under-mixed residual matrices stay close to the vertices of the Birkhoff polytope, hyper-connections will degenerate into a set of nearly independent streams; when over-mixed residual matrices are concentrated around the uniform barycenter of the polytope, streams will become less distinguishable from each other. We theoretically validate our findings by bounding the cumulative residual mixing, stream difference and gradient difference of mixing coefficients across layers. Motivated by these findings, we propose Contraction for Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (cmHC), an exact doubly stochastic method which geometrically contracts the Birkhoff polytope toward its interior. This contraction mitigates under-mixing by forcing the residual matrices to explore deeper into the polytope, with a provable stream mixing bound and no new parameterization. Moreover, we apply a moderate contraction at the shallow layers to prevent over-mixing, enabling stable gradient propagation on extremely large-scale model pretraining. Extensive experiments across dense and mixture-of-experts models at various parameter scales demonstrate the effectiveness and scalability of cmHC. We also develop fused operators for cmHC on Ascend NPUs, reducing hyper-connection execution latency by up to 44.8%.

AI Summary

▼ Abstract (click to expand)

Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash We study a new problem of preventing object penetration in interactive video world models. To understand how existing models handle this, we introduce NoPeBench, a benchmark that evaluates how world models respond to physical obstacles. Our evaluation reveals that none of the open-source models can reliably prevent such failures. Surprisingly, however, these models already encode the information needed to predict such violations. We find that the hidden states of diffusion transformers (DiTs) can predict whether a candidate action will penetrate an obstacle. Building on this, we introduce NoPeWorld, a lightweight framework that detects and prevents penetration. Specifically, we train a small prediction head on top of a frozen DiT's hidden states to read out if a candidate action is safe or not. Unsafe actions are then replaced with safe ones that best preserve the original intent. Despite its simplicity, NoPeWorld substantially reduces the physical violation rate, averaged over six video world models, from 74% to 16%. Remarkably, our method shows zero-shot generalization to open-domain imagery, third-person viewpoints, and moving obstacles. We publicly release our code, the NoPeBench benchmark, and model weights. HF Page arXiv PDF
AI Summary
Abstract Future-OPSD trains vision-language models via on-policy self-distillation and a contrastive privilege advantage derived from a foresight score, improving video foresight prediction and planning without extra annotations or LLM supervision. Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash
▼ Abstract (click to expand)

Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash Video foresight reasoning requires vision-language models to anticipate future events or next actions from currently observed videos. Existing methods fit future captions, rationales, or full visual states, but face two limitations. First, they rely on annotations or external large models for supervision, limiting scalability. We address this with an on-policy self-distillation framework that turns past-future video pairs into a natural self-supervised signal without extra annotations or LLM supervision. Second, they treat the whole future clip as equally valuable, although most of it is irrelevant context information. We address this by defining a foresight score, from which we obtain a contrastive privilege advantage that drives the model to learn the most valuable future information. Since the score is intractable, we optimize a tractable lower-bound surrogate that interpolates this advantage, computed between the true and a random reference future, with a teacher-student distillation advantage; together, they amplify decision-relevant content information and suppress irrelevant context information. We call the resulting framework Future-OPSD. On open-ended prediction and closed-ended planning, Future-OPSD improves base models by up to 14.2 and 15.9 points without compromising general video understanding. Code will be available at https://jingchensun.github.io/future-opsd/

AI Summary
Abstract We introduce Clover-35B-A3B-Preview (Clover), a mixture-of-experts diffusion language model with 35 billion total parameters and 3 billion activated parameters, supporting thinking-mode generation. Clover adapts Gated Delta Networks to block diffusion by combining recurrent memory of previously generated context with bidirectional softmax attention inside the current block. Historical memory readout competes with current-block tokens in a shared softmax, with learnable block-size calibration and a separate, zero-initialized right-context convolution branch. We organize the evaluation around model quality, drafting and verification costs, and deployment under constrained memory. We also describe a decision-model variant that generates answers to related questions in one denoising pass. On an internal set of 500 groups, joint prediction raises question accuracy from 90.37% to 93.88% and all-correct group accuracy from 65.4% to 77.0%.

MemOnce: A Scalable Memory Architecture for Large Language Models

Institution: Nanjing University
Authors: Chang Nie, Chaoyou Fu, Songlin Liu, Yiqing Hu, Caifeng Shan
HF Page arXiv PDF
AI Summary
Abstract Deploying large language models (LLMs) in long-horizon and continual interactions requires maintaining reusable memory states for future inference, posing challenges for existing architectures. Standard Transformers accumulate layer-wise, token-level KV caches, incurring linear GPU memory growth and quadratic computational complexity as the context expands. In contrast, linear-time architectures compress history into fixed-size states, but struggle to retain fine-grained details. We introduce MemOnce, a foundation architecture that learns scalable memory states for language models. MemOnce is based on two core designs: (a) Memory encoder-decoder: it caches only a small subset of final-layer hidden states as compact memory representations and dynamically decodes them back into KV caches for attention layers; (b) Memory sparse attention: for each continual input, MemOnce adaptively retrieves relevant memory representations, with the sparse indices computed only once across layers. Coupling these two designs enables efficient on-the-fly memory loading and KV cache decoding, drastically reducing the GPU memory footprint and achieving linear-time complexity. We conduct extensive experiments to show that MemOnce outperforms Transformers and other architectures across varying model sizes, context lengths, and training paradigms (pretraining and post-training adaptation). In particular, adapting Qwen3.5-4B with MemOnce reduces memory state cache by 1313times and prefill latency by 6.9times at 1M context, and improves throughput by 3.5times and overall memory performance by +5.77%. Finally, MemOnce generalizes zero-shot to downstream representation tasks, achieving competitive results against specialized models.

Reading the Unreadable: Text-Aware Image Super-Resolution Needs Reasoning

Institution: N/A
Authors: Jaeseong Lee, Jinwoo Kim, Jinho Jeong, Seon Joo Kim
HF Page arXiv PDF
AI Summary
Abstract Text-aware image super-resolution (TAISR) aims to recover high-resolution images from low-resolution inputs while preserving textual content. Existing methods treat this as a visual restoration problem, yet severely degraded text often cannot be read from local visual evidence alone. Humans instead read it by reasoning over image context, logical patterns, and prior knowledge. This raises a fundamental question: Does TAISR need reasoning? To answer this, we introduce two diagnostic tools. The first, ReasonText, is a human-curated benchmark that separates reasoning-required text from locally recoverable text. The second, Ground-Truth Text-Crop Accuracy (GTTCA), is a recognition-based metric that measures text restoration quality without text detection errors. Evaluating twelve recent super-resolution (SR) models, we find a consistent and substantial performance drop on reasoning-required text, revealing a systematic blind spot. Two observations point to a remedy. One is that text-conditioned SR models restore text substantially better when given ground-truth text. The other is that multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can read degraded text in low-resolution images through reasoning. We therefore propose Reasoning Transfer via Captioning (RTC), a training-free plug-in that uses an external MLLM to infer the degraded text and feeds the resulting caption to a text-conditioned SR model. RTC consistently improves all six text-conditioned SR models we evaluate, especially on reasoning-required text. Surprisingly, a real-world SR model with RTC can even outperform the TAISR model with RTC, suggesting that reasoning should be a core design axis for future TAISR.

Institution: N/A

AI Summary
Abstract Full-duplex interaction models exhibit strong conversational agency, enabling natural, low-latency turn-taking, backchannels, and interruptions. However, completing tasks further requires task agency: timely tool invocation and argument revision following user corrections. Frontend-backend agents separate initiation from execution: the frontend decides whether and what to request, while the backend determines how. Execution can be offloaded, but unsent requests cannot be executed, and incomplete ones can misdirect the backend. On Full-Duplex-Bench v3 (FDB v3), most failures of Realtime-Venus-Audio + Harness in our streaming pipeline occur before a domain tool executes. We introduce Duopra, a full-duplex interaction model that learns task execution alongside conversational control. Duopra treats the backend as a tool, accepting a self-contained natural-language request. Direct calls and backend delegation share an action interface and requirements for faithful initiation and correct execution. Its three-stage training combines protocol SFT, full-duplex RL rewarding timely and complete execution, and projector-only repair of talker conditioning after thinker updates. With the same backend, Pass@1 rises from 4.7% to 44.7%; Duopra achieves 47.7% calling tools directly (26.0% after SFT) and 49.3% with a DeepSeek backend. It improves three FDB v1.0 takeover rates over the base model. These results show that task agency can coexist with key conversational agency.

Lost in the Hallway: Benchmarking Persistent Spatial Memory Across Revisits

Institution: Tsinghua-LeapLab
Authors: Henry Zheng, Weibo Gong, Yang Yue, Rui Huang, Yang Zhang
HF Page arXiv PDF
AI Summary
Abstract Persistent spatial understanding is essential for embodied agents to operate reliably in environments they repeatedly encounter. It requires recognizing revisited places despite viewpoint changes, distinguishing them from similar-looking rooms, and updating memories as environments change. Yet existing evaluations often leave physical place identity across separated visits implicit. We introduce ReSpaceBench, a benchmark comprising 3,244 questions across 590 synthetic and real-home scenes, organized into 9 tasks spanning room association, persistent state tracking, and downstream reasoning. Controlled walkthroughs define visits by physical room entry and deliberately include same-type room distractors and cross-session object changes. Our evaluation of 32 models and 3 agentic video-understanding frameworks reveals substantial limitations: the strongest evaluated zero-shot model achieves 50.5% overall accuracy and 52.2% on a 450-question subset, compared with 90.1% for human annotators. The evaluated agentic frameworks offer no overall improvement over direct inference with the same base model. Analysis of agent execution traces reveals evidence omissions and incorrect cross-visit room associations as major failure modes, suggesting that iterative evidence seeking alone does not ensure consistent spatial memory. Further analyses expose a gap between recognizing familiar rooms and identifying the correct visited room, while annotation-guided sampling highlights the importance of preserving intervening visits and transitions. We also provide RS-30K, a separate training dataset from synthetic sources with no template, scene, route, or video overlap with the benchmark. Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B raises overall accuracy from 35.5% to 60.4%, with the largest gains in association and improvements extending to real-life scenes.

Existing VLMs typically infer 3D scene structure from one or more images without known camera calibration or cross-view geometric constraints. This makes metric 3D inference inherently ambiguous and leaves it unclear whether their spatial predictions are grounded in visual evidence or driven primarily by semantic priors. Seeing the Invisible: How Vision-Language Models Read, Predict and Trace Radio Fields

Institution: Nanyang Technological University
Authors: Yiyang Zhu, Xin Li, Guangyuan Liu, Ziheng Liu, Jiayi Zhang
HF Page arXiv PDF
AI Summary
Abstract Operating in the physical world requires reasoning beyond what cameras directly capture. In a city, radio signals are invisible to the lens, yet their strength is governed by the buildings and streets an image does show. Given only these visible structures, can vision-language models (VLMs) infer the invisible radio field they shape? Probing true physical reasoning requires exact fields in real cities, alongside controlled interventions where a single physical factor changes. We build a label-free engine that turns any coordinate on Earth into a ray-traceable city, simulates matched single-factor counterfactuals, and computes every answer exactly. This yields RadioVISTA, a benchmark and training dataset for visual reasoning about invisible fields. Physically, a radio field is the sum of its propagation paths, yet across six continents we uncover a striking field-path dissociation: frontier VLMs accurately judge where a field is strong, but fail to tell whether a single direct or one-wall path exists. Open models fail even earlier at reading the field itself, and are systematically wrong, scoring below random guessing even when credited for any of four attempts. On unseen cities, fine-tuning brings a 9B open model close to frontier proprietary models on fields, yet neither supervised nor reinforcement fine-tuning improves path tracing. Supervising only final answers teaches models to approximate the field, yet leaves single-path geometry out of reach.

From Points to Trajectories: Rethinking Multimodal Alignment for Physical Events

Institution: Nanyang Technological University
Authors: Yiyang Zhu, Guangyuan Liu, Jingdi Lei, Li Wei, Chau Yuen
HF Page arXiv PDF
AI Summary
Abstract Advancing physical artificial intelligence (Physical AI) requires moving beyond static object recognition to ground how real-world mechanics (e.g., impact, friction, and force transfer) unfold over time. While multimodal aligners seek to capture these interactions by matching multi-sensor streams, prevailing architectures compress each temporal sequence into a single pooled embedding. We show that this paradigm fails on twin events: distinct physical executions recorded under identical task conditions. Collapsing the temporal dimension averages out transient cross-modal dynamics, effectively reducing physical event alignment to static scene recognition. Formally, we prove that as sequence length increases, any temporally pooled representation converges to the stationary recording condition. Consequently, as windows grow, no choice of loss function or embedding geometry can disambiguate twin events from pooled features alone. Instead, we show that physical events are uniquely identified by the event gap, namely the instantaneous, synchronous co-fluctuations across modalities that are erased by pooling and accumulate only along unpooled trajectories. To recover this signal, we introduce CounterPoint, a lightweight plug-in module that strips out each modality's temporal mean to score frame-level cross-modal agreement. Integrated across four state-of-the-art (SOTA) multimodal aligners, CounterPoint shifts representations from coarse condition matching to fine-grained physical event alignment, consistently outperforming existing temporal alignment methods.

AI Summary
Abstract Frontier multimodal language agents such as GPT-6 Astra have demonstrated strong capabilities in controlling robots directly via low-level numerical actions without high-level primitives. However, existing benchmarks report only episode-level success rates, which show neither how these agents succeed nor where they fail. In this paper, we present a systematic action-level analysis of frontier agents on direct robot control across 12 manipulation benchmarks. AeroRSI: Recursive Self-Improvement for Aerial Vision-Language Navigation

Institution: Tsinghua University
Authors: DavidWu1116, Shiwei Wu, Yuwen Pan, Zhi Li, Yaowen Li
HF Page arXiv PDF
AI Summary
Abstract Vision-language-action (VLA) models enable unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to follow natural-language instructions, yet still fail frequently in closed-loop flight. Correcting these failures requires repairs, i.e., actions that avoid them at the states the policy itself reaches, which human pilots cannot label at scale and rule-based planners cannot supply reliably. In this paper, we present AeroRSI, a recursive self-improvement framework in which an aerial navigation policy iteratively learns from its own failures. Our key insight is that a failing policy should judge its own repairs by flying on from them. Judged in this way, its sampled actions and even rule-based proposals that hurt it when imitated directly become reliable supervision. Moreover, an episode terminates at its first failure, concealing subsequent failures until the earlier ones are repaired. AeroRSI thus renews its supervision in every round, anchoring the failures of the current policy, verifying repairs by its continuation, and distilling them while keeping its behavior elsewhere. On TravelUAV, three rounds raise the success rate of a supervised 2B-parameter policy from 46.4% to 67.5% and reduce collisions by half, surpassing the previous state of the art by 10.6 points from the same initialization without extra human labels or inference cost. Code and models are available at https://github.com/starsholic/AeroRSI.

Two records may match at the base level but not at the variant level: they share an underlying design while differing in attributes such as color or size. Final matching accuracy does not separately characterize the capabilities involved. To study this problem, we use e-commerce as a challenging real-world domain. How Feedback-Free Self-Evolution Reshapes Agent Skills: A Systematic Study

Institution: N/A
Authors: Litao Guo, Man Chen, Xinli Xu, Jinsong Zhou, Zixin Zhang
HF Page arXiv PDF
AI Summary
Abstract Agent skills are documents in which experts write down procedural knowledge, and models increasingly maintain them by rewriting them without feedback. Such rewriting could adapt a skill to the model that uses it or erode the expert knowledge that makes it useful, yet how skills evolve under this feedback-free self-evolution has not been studied. We let models rewrite expert skills round after round without any task, run or judgment and run agents built on the same models with the rewritten skills. Our experiments show that the skills lose value rather than improve, as every model we test performs worse with them on every benchmark, by 15 points on average on SkillsBench after thirty rounds. Sampling each model's prior with skills it writes from the topic alone, we find that rewriting pulls skills toward this prior and gives up first the knowledge outside it. Putting this knowledge back recovers much of the lost pass rate, so an expert's skill is valuable largely for what the model does not write on its own. Building on this insight, SkillDiff marks this knowledge in place and raises the pass rate on SkillsBench by 4.8 points on average without feedback or any change to the skill's text. Institution: EPFL

AI Summary
Abstract Modern language models (LMs) are increasingly deployed with access to search tools at inference. Yet pretraining remains predominantly focused on predicting static text, with search capabilities typically introduced during post-training. We propose Search-Native Pretraining as a practical route to teach search capability during pretraining from ordinary text corpora. Specifically, we augment pretraining corpus at scale with web-search queries and retrieve web response using a local index. Throughout our experiments, we find that a 1.4B QueryLM consistently beats or matches the performance of a FLOP-matched 3.3B baseline model across all knowledge intensive tasks.
