TeleChat to Xing4: Advancing Frontier Agentic Models on Domestic Chips and Frameworks

Institution: XingChen-AGI
Authors: Xingchen AGI Lab, Baotian Hu, Bingkai Yang, Boyang Wang, Changzai Pan
HF Page arXiv PDF
AI Summary
Abstract We present Xing 4, a series of large language models that extends the TeleChat lineage through coordinated advances in model architecture and training infrastructure on Ascend. This report describes the design principles and systems engineering behind the series, spanning architecture selection, large-scale pretraining, and reasoning and agentic post-training. We study how sparse activation, attention design, model depth and width, and speculative decoding balance model capacity with computation, memory access, and execution overhead. Our pretraining infrastructure builds on MindSpore HyperParallel, combining topology-aware hybrid sharding, efficient distributed optimizer execution, and coordinated management of long-sequence workloads and activation memory. CPU affinity and core isolation further reduce host scheduling interference. Together, these mechanisms support the pretraining of a 285B-parameter model on 8,192 Ascend 910C cards, providing a concrete setting for the architectural and systems studies. For post-training, we introduce TeleGym, a unified agent execution platform that connects execution, evaluation, and trajectory construction to policy optimization. Disaggregated asynchronous execution, bounded oversampling, and partial rollout accommodate heterogeneous task durations while preserving trajectory continuity across policy updates. Together, these advances establish an Ascend-oriented training foundation for the continued development of Xing 4 across model scales and increasingly demanding reasoning and agent workloads.

AI Summary
Abstract When large language models write neural network weights without gradient-based training, they tend to design sparse table or associative-memory models that outperform same-sized Transformers with far fewer parameters and faster GPU inference, though pretrained LLMs remain more accurate. Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash
▼ Abstract (click to expand)

Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash Standard neural architectures such as the Transformer were chosen because they train well with gradient descent and run fast on GPUs. In weight-space programming, a large language model (LLM) writes target weights by hand without gradient-based training, so those reasons no longer apply. Should we keep the Transformer? Given minimal guidance, ten labeled examples, and a sandbox, mainly on multiple-choice questions, we task three frontier LLMs with either generating the weights of a fixed architecture or designing the architecture themselves. When free to design, all three compile plain-text facts into a sparse table queried by a fixed procedure, reaching up to 54.4% test accuracy. Without gradients, they choose a table, in which the LLM can state what each weight means and can find and rewrite a wrong one. Each wrong answer points to one row, and removing a row changes about one other answer. From this, we propose an associative-memory-based model up to 17x faster on GPUs than a same-sized Transformer. We also test pretrained weights, other benchmarks, and tasks where models generate answers. Pretrained LLMs remain more accurate; the claim concerns which structure to build when writing weights.

AI Summary

▼ Abstract (click to expand)

Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash Transformer language models are commonly scaled by making their blocks wider or stacking them deeper. In this work, we explore a complementary path: branching, the parallel composition of full Transformer blocks. We introduce the Multi-Branch Transformer (MBT), which processes the input through shared initial layers, executes independently parameterized branches of sequential blocks in parallel, and fuses their outputs before shared final layers. At comparable training compute, we study three MBT variants: Wide-Shallow preserves block width, Narrow-Deep preserves sequential depth, and Balanced interpolates between them. In controlled pretraining experiments at four mixture-of-experts scales (0.8B to 2.1B total parameters), 2-branch Wide-Shallow MBTs consistently outperform sequential Transformers under matched parameter and training-compute budgets. Balanced MBTs improve further and retain comparable quality as the number of branches increases. To improve inference efficiency at long contexts, we introduce Joint-KV, which constructs a shared KV cache from branch representations at each depth. Our final recipe combines Balanced MBTs with Joint-KV, supported by an inference engine that exploits branch grouping and shared-cache reuse. Fitted scaling curves indicate that standard Transformers require up to 1.26x as much training body compute to match our final models' validation loss. These models reduce full-model KV-cache storage by up to 34% and deliver up to 51.4% higher decoding throughput on a single H200 GPU. Ablation studies show that branching yields gains beyond changes in width and depth, enables an effective KV-sharing strategy (Joint-KV), remains robust across fusion functions, and performs best in the middle layers. These findings show that branching offers a practical path for scaling language models toward both better modeling quality and faster inference.

ForceHarness: Turning Force into Action and Physics for Frozen Frontier Models

Institution: Show Lab
Authors: Jack97, Ke Sun, Kevin Yuchen Ma, Yike Zhang, Yanzhe Chen
HF Page arXiv PDF
AI Summary
Abstract Frontier multimodal models can now control robots zero-shot from images, yet many manipulation tasks depend on contact forces that images cannot show. Bringing force to an API-only frozen model raises two challenges. First, contact evolves much faster than an API model can decide, so the system must close the loop on force beneath the model. Second, a frozen model cannot interpret raw force streams, so force must be returned in a form the model can reason about. Therefore, we present ForceHarness, a harness that lets frozen models use force without demonstrations or finetuning. After calibrating hand force from a wrist sensor or joint torques alone, ForceHarness addresses the first challenge with self-terminating force primitives that regulate force on their own and return at a contact event. To address the second challenge, a physics translation layer turns each force trace into physical quantities with uncertainty as the tool result. To evaluate ForceHarness against expert force controllers and learned policies under identical conditions, we also build HiddenPhys, a seven-task MuJoCo benchmark with hidden physical variables. On a real arm without a wrist force sensor, ForceHarness raises the zero-shot success rate of GPT-6 Astra from 3.3% to 90.0%. In simulation, it adds 17.5 percentage points on the public ForceBench suite and 34.3 percentage points on HiddenPhys, surpassing force-aware policies trained on demonstrations on both benchmarks and matching hand-written expert controllers on HiddenPhys. Ablations further show that smaller models make significantly better decisions when given physical quantities rather than raw force values.

Abstract Autoregressive video diffusion models generate frames based on new observations and inputs, making them a promising medium for interactive visual experiences. However, existing models are orders of magnitude too slow for fluid interaction. This paper introduces FastFrames, a family of fast autoregressive video diffusion models that achieves interactive latency without compromising quality. Abstract Asynchronous reinforcement learning increases training throughput, but data from stale policies and numerical mismatch between training and inference can destabilize learning. To stabilize off-policy learning, score centering (SC) removes the effect of reward baselines on the expected update.
