Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash System One models promise fast, general-purpose decision-making without lengthy token generation. However, understanding their capability spectrum and decision reliability requires more than aggregate performance on current benchmarks. Existing evaluations often provide limited insight into the breadth of model capabilities and the reliability of their decisions. We introduce SO-Arena, a benchmark characterizing the capability spectrum and decision reliability of System One models across 11 domains and five capability families. For the capability spectrum, SO-Arena offers evidence-grounded evaluation through reliable sources and manual verification of reference labels against model-visible inputs; fine-grained capability assessment through domain-specific difficulty criteria; and realistic interactive evaluation, where models iteratively execute actions in sandboxes. Reasoning-intensive tasks further enable system one-two comparison. Beyond accuracy, SO-Arena assesses decision reliability through two complementary measures: decision stability under controlled option-set changes and missing-answer recognition when no valid option is available. Evaluations across 21 models reveal that the benefits of scaling and additional reasoning vary substantially across domains. More importantly, strong overall performance does not necessarily imply reliable decision-making: the best-performing System One model ranks only seventh in reliability. Together, these findings highlight the need to characterize capability spectrum and decision reliability as complementary dimensions, providing a systematic view of the strengths and limitations of System One models.

BirdDreams: Joint Perspective and BEV Video Generation for a Plausible, Reactive Driving World Model

Institution: NVIDIA
Authors: Junyoung Seo, Jaewoo Seo, Seungryong Kim, Laura Leal-Taixé, Xiuming Zhang
HF Page arXiv PDF
AI Summary
Abstract Driving world models aim to simulate how a scene unfolds under different ego actions. This requires plausible motion of surrounding vehicles as well as precise ego-trajectory control. Learning vehicle motion from perspective video alone is challenging because motion is entangled with appearance and vehicles can be occluded. Prior work therefore conditions video generation on layouts that specify vehicle motion, providing control over surrounding vehicles but requiring their future trajectories as inputs. We introduce BirdDreams, an autoregressive driving world model that jointly generates perspective video and a schematic bird's-eye-view (BEV) video, making scene layout part of the predicted future. The BEV, rendered from scene annotations for training, depicts roads and vehicles as simple shapes at a fixed metric scale, providing a top-down view of their positions and motion with reduced occlusion between vehicles. Perspective video, by contrast, retains scene details that the BEV omits, such as traffic signals and object appearance. Joint attention between two video diffusion transformers, one per stream, lets each stream inform the other during generation. The predicted BEV also provides a geometric interface for ego control: we translate and rotate its latest frame along an input ego trajectory and use the resulting frames as a soft condition that shows how the scene would appear to move if only the ego vehicle moved. At inference, BirdDreams generates both videos from a single camera image without requiring an HD map or vehicle-state inputs. On PhysicalAI-AV and nuScenes, BirdDreams follows input ego trajectories more faithfully than prior driving world models and generates more plausible ego motion without ego-action conditioning. Human evaluation shows that BirdDreams outperforms a perspective-only baseline in generating realistic surrounding-vehicle behavior.

G-STAR: Group-Scaled Token Capacity for Deeply Compressed Autoregressive Generation

Institution: LongCat
Authors: Xu Huang, Ye Huang, Xiaotong Li, Zhengrong Yue, Jialv Zou
HF Page arXiv PDF
AI Summary
Abstract Discrete autoregressive models offer a scalable framework for image generation. However, they face a fundamental trade-off: long token sequences preserve representation quality but slow generation, while aggressive spatial compression reduces representation capacity. Based on product quantization, we show that, under a fixed nominal information budget, group size should scale quadratically with the per-axis spatial compression factor. Building on this principle, we propose GSTAR, a spatial--group co-scaling framework that enables \(32\times\) and even effective \(64\times\) compression while preserving strong reconstruction, understanding, and generation performance. Since larger groups introduce intra-group dependencies that must be modeled efficiently, we analyze their impact on inference latency and generation quality and introduce a semi-autoregressive decoder that combines parallel feature computation with lightweight causal modeling. With these designs, our highly compressed models perform competitively with leading models across five representative text-to-image benchmarks (GenEval, DPG-Bench, PRISM, LongText-Bench, and Qwen-Image-Bench). At \(1024\times1024\) resolution, our model delivers a \(20\times\) speedup over prior discrete autoregressive models, generating each image in 2.5 seconds on a single NVIDIA H200. Together, these results show that highly compressed discrete representations can enable high-quality autoregressive generation at low latency.

Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash In this report, we introduce the Reciprocal World Model (RWM) and present Duo v1, a full-duplex world model that continuously perceives audiovisual inputs while responding to users in real time. The RWM treats intelligence and physical reality as equally fundamental to what constitutes a world. Existing world models largely focus on simulating the latter, leaving reasoning and decision-making as external controls rather than intrinsic parts of the world itself. In RWM, we identify two equally fundamental capabilities: Intent, which provides the cognition underlying world interactions, and Realization, which generates coherent audio-visual behavior. To realize this formulation, Duo v1 introduces three key technical designs: (1) Leveraging autoregressive modeling for continuous interaction reasoning and participation decisions, rather than restricting it to instruction understanding for diffusion models. (2) Constructing chunk-specific conditions and synchronize tokens across Intent and Realization on a unified timeline. Module-specific attention masks enforce causal dependencies both within and across chunks, preventing access to future inputs. (3) Mitigating distribution shift in both modules through dual rollout distillation, training on autoregressively generated Intent tokens and synthesized audiovisual histories from Realization. Both modules continuously perceive real-time audio, video, and text, enabling full-duplex interaction with spontaneous conversations and interruptions. Beyond conversation, users can share real objects, reshape virtual scenes, guide assistants, or play audiovisual games without mouse or keyboard controls. The release of Duo v1 makes our first step, with the next version coming soon. We look forward to broader exploration of this emerging field.

Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash Hyper-Connections (HC) architectures are increasingly adopted in frontier language models, yet their components remain insufficiently understood and introduce non-negligible training overhead. In this work, we systematically study the HC architecture to understand its components and identify simpler, more efficient designs. We find that learned read/write gating without mixing and unconstrained mixing with fixed gates form two effective interaction groups: either alone achieves performance comparable to mHC. We also identify repeated access to the expanded residual state as a major source of training overhead, even without mixing. Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash How multimodal language models should encode visual input remains up for debate. Most models use a pretrained vision encoder, while some recent recipes train the encoder from scratch with the language model or remove it entirely. Vision encoders bundle together three design choices: a visual prior from upstream pretraining, dedicated vision capacity, and pre-fusion placement of that capacity (i.e., before the multimodal backbone). Because published models also differ in data, architecture, and compute, comparing them cannot isolate the effect of each choice. We disentangle these choices via a controlled framework where we pretrain all models from scratch on common data and compare them on visual question answering and language benchmarks at matched lifetime compute. We find that the visual prior is not necessary; an encoder trained jointly from scratch is on par with a pretrained one, without a separate training stage. What matters instead is dedicated vision capacity: an encoder-free model trails on visual understanding even when its backbone is enlarged by the encoder's parameter count. Yet that capacity need not precede multimodal fusion, as vision-only modules added in-fusion to early backbone layers perform comparably to a pre-fusion encoder and can lower time to first token. These results also hold across backbone sizes, when encoder pretraining is treated as free, and when starting from a pretrained language model. Overall, our findings favor dedicated vision capacity trained jointly with the backbone and placed early within it.

Generated by deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash Recent work shows that multimodal agents increasingly self-improve without recurring human annotation. However, each self-improvement mechanism is designed and evaluated within a single scenario, leaving its preconditions unverified under cross-scenario transfer. This survey investigates the structural source of asymmetric mechanism transfer across four scenarios: multimodal reasoning, multimodal generation, GUI and Web navigation, and embodied manipulation. We first formalize the self-evolving multimodal agent by four criteria and a differentia of self-reference, and adopt the three-stage self-evolving loop of experience generation, feedback acquisition, and update consolidation as our analytical framework to position the scenarios along ceilings on rollout count and feedback fidelity. Comparing self-evolving with fixed loops within each scenario separates the limitations that the scenario imposes from those inherent in the self-evolving design. We then recast each mechanism as a trade-off that raises a scarce factor by presupposing a task-structural resource, such as output equivalence or checkable outcomes, and judge it portable, conditional, blocked, or redundant across scenarios. We attribute the asymmetry to divergent combinations of scarce factors and resources across opposite transfer directions, and identify the matched-budget measurements that could overturn these still untested verdicts.
